# One cross, two descriptions — the describer's per-side variance

*Read 2026-08-15 on the founder's own production crops, read-only, no spend.
Ordered by fable-590 §2 and sharpened by his eyewitness (fable-591): he says the
pair was matched. Artifact: `output/earring-crops/pair.png`.*

---

## The fork this settles

His render 193 ("give her horns and dangly cross earrings") filed both earrings
with crops, and the panel's two rows read differently:

```
left    "Small silver cross pendant on a thin silver chain, plain narrow crucifix…"
right   "Silver-tone cross pendant with rounded tubular arms and beveled edges…"
```

Two readings were possible: the frame genuinely held two different crosses (the
records would then be HONEST), or one object was described twice, differently.

## The crops say: one object

```
earring@left    35×106 px
earring@right   34×111 px
```

Side by side they are the same cross — the same tubular arms with beveled
edges, the same hoop-and-chain, the same proportions, the 5% difference in
height being what perspective does across a face. **His eye was right, and now
it is exact.**

Both descriptions are TRUE of that object. They emphasise different things: one
notices the chain and the narrow crucifix, the other the tubular arms and the
bevel. Neither is wrong; they are two answers to one question.

## Why it matters for the drift

The words are what a later render carries when a crop does not ride. Two
different sentences about one object invite two different paintings of it —
which is exactly what he saw next: **both crosses drifted, the right one more.**

So this is a describer-consistency finding rather than a carry one, and it sits
underneath the carry question rather than replacing it.

## The rule, with the founder's own condition on it

*(fable-591 §1, amended by fable-592 — his words: "the description can genuinely
be different if I edit the left or right earring to genuinely be a different
earring, or ask for 2 different earrings in the first place.")*

```
MATCHED pair     → per-side words that AGREE       (his 193 case: the defect)
MISMATCHED pair  → per-side words that DIFFER,     (per-side independence is
                   each matching its own side       the feature)
```

**Both arms are mandatory in any fix's court.** A consistency patch that forces
agreement onto a deliberate mismatch is the worse bug: it would erase the very
per-side independence the pair machinery exists to give him.

## The limits

- **One pair, one render, one face.** Two crops looked at.
- **Nothing here measures the drift itself** — only that the two objects
  described differently were one object.
- **The describer's own calls were not re-run.** Whether it varies like this
  every time, or varied once, is a population question this note does not
  answer.
