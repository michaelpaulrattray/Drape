INTENDED USE

A blank template asset for a tattoo "flash sheet" system — the ARM sheet. A
tattoo that wraps around an arm must be drawn on several views of that same arm
on one sheet, so the design stays consistent from any angle (like a character
turnaround sheet). The whole value of this asset is that the FOUR VIEWS ARE
VISIBLY DIFFERENT ROTATIONS OF ONE ARM. A previous attempt produced four views
that all looked like the same view repeated; that is the failure to avoid.
It must contain NO tattoo, NO design, NO ink and NO text of any kind.

WHAT TO PRODUCE

One image containing FOUR views of the SAME single neutral, featureless ghost
mannequin LEFT ARM, hanging straight down, upright side by side in a row, evenly
spaced. It is one arm turned through four quarter rotations — a turnaround —
not four different arms and not the same view four times.

THE FRAME OF EACH VIEW — this is what makes the rotation readable

Each view runs from the top of the shoulder (deltoid) down THROUGH THE ELBOW and
a short way onto the upper forearm. THE ELBOW MUST BE FULLY VISIBLE AND IN
FOCUS IN ALL FOUR VIEWS — it is the one landmark that tells a viewer which way
the arm is facing, and it must not be swallowed by the soft fade at the bottom.
No hand, no wrist, no torso, no neck.

WHAT EACH VIEW MUST SHOW, IN THIS ORDER LEFT TO RIGHT

  1  OUTER (lateral) side — the rounded cap of the deltoid sits at the top and
     the outer contour of the arm is one long, almost straight line from
     shoulder to elbow. Biceps and triceps are both seen edge-on, so neither
     bulges toward the viewer. At the bottom, the bony outer point of the elbow
     is a small ridge on the silhouette.

  2  FRONT (anterior) — the biceps is a clear soft bulge in the middle of the
     upper arm, facing the viewer, tapering into a shallow hollow just above the
     elbow. The inner crease of the elbow (the shallow triangular dip on the
     front of the joint) is visible at the bottom. The overall silhouette is
     widest at the middle and narrows toward the joint.

  3  INNER (medial) side — the flattest and softest of the four. A long shallow
     groove runs down the length of the arm between the front and back muscle
     masses. No prominent bulge anywhere; the surface is smooth and even, and
     the arm reads as narrower here than in view 1.

  4  BACK (posterior) — the triceps forms a broad horseshoe-shaped mass in the
     lower half of the upper arm, and immediately below it the point of the
     elbow is a distinct rounded knob standing out from the silhouette. This is
     the view with the strongest form at the bottom.

A person who cannot see any labels must be able to look at the four and say
which is the front and which is the back. If two of the four look the same,
the image has failed.

CRITICAL RULES

- It is a MANNEQUIN limb, not a person's arm: no hair, no veins, no freckles,
  no moles, no jewellery, no clothing, no fingernails. Smooth, anonymous,
  sculptural, with just enough soft shading to read the muscle form.
- All four views are IDENTICAL in size, skin tone, lighting direction and
  vertical alignment — the same arm, the same lamp, four rotations.
- MATTE and LOW-CONTRAST: soft even studio light, no gloss, no harsh shadow, no
  rim light, no cast shadow on the background.
- The SHOULDER at the TOP and the FOREARM at the BOTTOM each TERMINATE IN A SOFT
  FADE that dissolves into the background. Never a hard cut, never a sliced
  edge, never a stand or plinth. The bottom fade begins BELOW the elbow, on the
  forearm, so that the elbow itself is fully drawn.
- Mid-tone skin, a neutral medium beige-brown, matte, identical across all four.

BACKGROUND AND COMPOSITION

- One flat, even NEAR-WHITE background across the whole image (a very light
  warm off-white, close to #F4F4F2 — clearly not pure white, clearly not grey),
  with no gradient and no vignette.
- THE FADES MUST DISAPPEAR INTO IT: no halo, no outline, no visible edge where
  each arm's soft termination ends.
- Landscape orientation, generous even margins, the four arms tidily aligned on
  a common top line and a common bottom line.
- No labels, no captions, no arrows, no numbers, no watermark, no logo, no
  borders, no letters or digits anywhere in the image.

AS SHIPPED — the one mechanical step after generation

The generated frame ran a long way past the elbow, down most of a forearm. The
placement vocabulary has no forearm in it (`neck`, `upper arm`, `upper chest`),
so that stretch is canvas the product cannot name. Two attempts to generate a
tighter frame were both worse — one lost the matte finish and came back as a
glossy anatomical study, the other lost the very rotation legibility this redo
exists to buy — so the shipped file is the good generation with its lower edge
cut and re-faded by hand:

  source   templates/source/ink-template-arm-nearwhite-generated.png (committed beside this)
  script   scripts/crop-ink-arm-template-disposable.mts (re-running it writes the same bytes)
  cut      row 800 of 1024
  fade     the 110 rows above the cut, dissolved linearly to the background
  bg       rgb(246,245,243), sampled from the frame's own corner (flat, no gradient)
  margin   96 rows of that background below the cut

No anatomy is manufactured by this step: it removes canvas and softens the new
edge to the frame's own background, which is what the generator's own
terminations do. Declared here, in the commit, and on the founder's card.
