# A tone carried from BELOW HER CHIN — the pre-flight

> **Status: dated record.** A measurement/evidence/court document from the date it states — it records what was true then; individual verdicts may since have been superseded. Current law: CLAUDE.md, the capability atlas, `DECISION_LOG.md` (#69 stamping sweep, 2026-08-28).


*Run 2026-08-15 on the tan court's own frames, already on disk. **$0.09 of house

Every bar below was written in the script's header before the first call.*

## Why this question is not the one that closed

Skin closed as a carrier (fable-423 §1) and it closed on a **face cut** — 1 of 3
survived, and the same ruling said why: *a carrier's identity cost scales with
how much of her facial GEOMETRY it contains.* The build carrier proved the other
half of that sentence in the same week: a below-head crop contains no face, and
it keeps 92–109% of a delivered build where words keep nothing at all.

So the cut nobody has tried is the one **below her chin**: her own skin, no
facial geometry, ridden as a PROPERTY sample — *"her skin tone, sampled from her
neck and shoulders"* — never as an EXTENT claim, which is the thing the
catalogue's `skin` note refuses and rightly (fable-409 §1).

Three readings decide whether a render bench is worth buying. A render bench is
about twenty generations; these cost nine cents, and any one of them could have
killed the road.

## B1 — is there anything to sample? **PASS, 7 of 7**

```
every frame     ~107,000px after a 12px erosion  ·  6.8% of the frame
the bar         >= 10,000px on at least 5 of 7
```

For scale: the face cut that skin closed on gave 68k–107k px. **The below-chin
sample is as large as the face cut at its largest, on every frame.**

And the artifact says what the number says. The sample painted onto magenta is
her neck, her upper chest and both upper arms — **no shirt, no background, no
face** — which is the reading that matters, because "below the chin" would have
been mostly t-shirt if the segmenter had answered the question loosely.

## B2 — does the tan even reach it? **PASS, 3 of 3**

This is the arm that could have ended the road outright: if the ask tans her
FACE only, a below-chin sample carries her ORIGINAL tone, and riding it would
erase the tan she paid for rather than hold it.

```
            below-chin moved      her face moved      bar: 3F = 2.79
tan-1            20.28                18.72
tan-2            19.04                15.95
tan-3            21.76                17.88
```

**The tan reaches below her chin more strongly than it reaches her face**, on
all three. The sample carries the thing it would be minted for.

## B3 — does the sample say the colour she is? **Reported raw, and it is small**

```
O = dE(her face skin, her below-chin skin), same frame
  master          3.07      untanned: the shadow under a jaw, and that is all it is
  tan-1/2/3       4.02  5.82  6.93
  chain-1/2/3     4.97  9.45  9.29
                            for scale, the tan's own signal is 17.51
```

On an untanned frame her neck reads **ΔE 3 from her face** — the offset is
lighting, it is small, and it is the correction term this carrier would have to
live with rather than a reason not to build it. It grows with the tan (the tan
lands slightly deeper below the chin) and grows again down the chain.

**The honest risk, stated before the bench rather than after:** hand the engine
a sample that reads ΔE 4–7 darker than her face and labelled *her skin tone*,
and it may pull her face toward the neck's shade. The render bench must read
that directly — the direction, not only the magnitude.

## And the drift the carrier would have to beat, measured here again

```
                below-chin drifted      her face drifted
pair 1               11.99                  10.74
pair 2               15.03                  10.78
pair 3               11.04                   8.13
```

Under words, one unrelated edit moves her below-chin skin **further than it
moves her face**. The region a carrier would pin is the region that moves most,
which is an argument for the carrier and a warning about what it has to hold.

## The limits

- **One face, one framing, one skin tone.** These are the tan court's frames:
  a head-and-shoulders portrait in a t-shirt. A closer crop, a polo neck or a
  scarf gives less below-chin skin or none, and B1 says nothing about those.
- **A sample is not a survival.** Everything here says the sample EXISTS and
  CARRIES THE TAN. Whether riding it holds the shade through a later edit is
  exactly what the closed face-cut bench measured and what the render bench must
  now measure — no number here predicts that.
- **The instrument answered the same on every phrasing.** "skin", "her skin" and
  "bare skin" agree within 0.2% (106,535 / 106,480 / 106,397 px eroded); "neck"
  returns a third of it. So the reading is not a lucky word.
