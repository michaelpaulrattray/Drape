# Freckles — the court, and why the door stayed shut

*Run 2026-08-15 against arms pre-registered in the script's header, ordered by

## The verdict: SHORT — the capability waits

```
CONTROL   freckles still there   1 of 3     ← the finding
REMOVAL   gone                   3 of 3
          skin clean             1 of 3     ← the second finding
SURVIVAL  still gone             3 of 3
```

**Fable's rule was survival, and survival passed. It is not what stopped this.**
Two other readings did, and either one alone would have been enough.

## 1. Freckles leave on their own — the control says so

Three renders of an unrelated edit (*"her t-shirt is black"*) with freckles
**never mentioned**, and they survived exactly one:

```
control/1   "clear, natural skin texture with no freckles"
control/2   "light scattered freckles on cheeks and nose"    ← the only survivor
control/3   "clear smooth skin, no freckles visible"
```

So a removal cannot be credited with taking off something that leaves by itself.
The removal arm's 3 of 3 is unattributable: two of those three would have gone
anyway.

**This is the tan's class, confirmed on a second specimen family.** The tan
drifts ΔE 10 per subsequent edit; freckles simply vanish. One is a surface that
cannot be HELD and the other is the same thing said louder — and Fable's own
sentence covers both: *a fact you can neither keep nor undo is worse than one
you can only keep.*

## 2. Where the removal did land, it damaged the skin

```
removal/1   "smooth, even-toned skin, no freckles visible"          clean
removal/2   "smooth, slightly waxy, airbrushed skin tone"           NOT clean
removal/3   "smooth, slightly airbrushed skin with no visible texture"  NOT clean
survival    all three: "smooth, slightly waxy/airbrushed"           NOT clean
```

The sentence was written specifically to prevent this — *"with its own natural
texture and colour rather than a flattened or airbrushed patch"* — and it
arrived anyway, in two of three removals and three of three survivals. **Words
did not hold a surface**, which is the same finding the carrier bench has been
circling and the first time it has been measured on a removal.

## What was done about it

The kind, its words and its sentence STAY — they are correct, and they are what
the next court will use. **`canDepart` is the door and it is shut**, checked at
the ask: a freckle removal refuses `uncatalogued` exactly as it did before this
slice, while the glasses and beard roads are untouched and still work (driven).

The scaffolding that was declared is therefore standing but inert, which is the
honest outcome of a court that came back short. Nothing about freckles ships.

## The boundary this finding does NOT cross (fable-539 §3)

**Freckles a face was BORN with are a different mechanism and are expected
safe.** They ride the master anchor as PIXELS — reference 1 is the photograph,
and the repaint reproduces it — while everything this court watched vanish was
recipe-carried: added by an edit and re-said in words on every later render.
The finding is about words failing to hold a surface, not about the master
failing to hold its own face.

Stated because the alternative is worse than an unmeasured gap: without this
line, the next reader of *"freckles vanish on their own"* would reasonably fear
for every born-freckled cast in production. With it, the fear has a boundary and
**the born case has a named, unbought measurement** — one that costs a bare
specimen and three renders whenever somebody wants it.

## What it would take to open the door

The surface carrier work — the same owner as the tan. Both specimens say the
same thing: a surface fact needs something other than words to persist, and
until it has one, *removing* it is not a capability the product can promise.
When that lands, this court re-runs unchanged: the specimen, the arms and the
sentence are all on disk (`output/freckle-court/`).

## The limits

- **n = 3 on one face**, one skin tone, freckles rendered on rather than born
  there. The control's 1-of-3 is the strongest reading here and it is still
  three renders.
- **The judge is one reader family.** Its `saw` strings are specific and
  consistent ("slightly waxy", "airbrushed"), which is what makes the
  skin-clean column readable at all, but a second reader was not bought.
- **The removal arm is unattributable rather than failed.** It may work
  perfectly; this court cannot say, because the control took the difference
  away.
