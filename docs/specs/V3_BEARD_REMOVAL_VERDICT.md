# A beard comes off — V3 slice (b)'s court

*Run 2026-08-15 against a design pre-registered in the script's own header
before the first call, ordered by fable-531 §4a. **Total spend: $1.09 of house
money** (10 renders, 10 reads). Ledger read at both ends: 96 rows → 96 rows, net
unchanged. No user credits, no rows written anywhere.*

**Specimen:** one of the founder's own production candidates — a man with light
stubble — with a full beard rendered onto him first, so the beard was
unmistakably there before anything was asked of it. Every frame is in
`output/beard-court/`, and the contact sheet reads left to right: bearded
master, control, removal, survival.

---

## The verdict, in one line

**A beard in the master can be removed, cleanly, and it stays removed** — 3 of 3
on every arm, including the one born-worn removals had already been measured
failing.

```
CONTROL   beard still there   3 of 3    the judge can SEE a beard
REMOVAL   gone                3 of 3
          skin clean          3 of 3
SURVIVAL  still gone          3 of 3    one more edit, the vacancy restated
```

## What was actually under test

The sentence, and it was **imported from the shipped module rather than
retyped** — a court that retypes the thing it is testing measures a sentence
that merely resembles the product's:

> *no beard, moustache or stubble — his jaw, chin and upper lip clean-shaven,
> the skin there the same tone as the rest of his face with no shadow or
> stubble texture*

Until this slice there was no such sentence for a beard, and the ask refused
`uncatalogued`: the phrase that says a thing is gone lived on the table for
things you WEAR.

## Why the control is the important arm

A judge that answered *"no beard"* about a bearded man would pass the removal
arm with nothing removed. So three renders were made with an unrelated edit and
facial hair **never mentioned**, and the beard was still there 3 of 3 — *"full
dark beard, evenly textured"*, *"full dark beard, even edges"*. The instrument
can say yes, which is what makes its no worth something.

Every affirmative carries its own `saw`; an answer without one is read as no
(D-235).

## Why the survival arm is the finding

`library-holds-presence-not-absence` was proved this month: a born-worn removal
**lasted exactly one frame**, because the next step re-anchored on the master
and painted the thing back. That is the failure this arm exists to catch, and it
did not happen here — a further edit (a grey scarf), with the vacancy restated
the way the recipe restates it, came back clean-shaven 3 of 3.

## The honest limits

- **n = 3 on ONE specimen, one beard, one skin tone.** The failure mode this
  court is about (the master repainting it) is structural rather than
  face-specific, but three renders on one man is what was bought.
- **The beard was RENDERED onto him rather than born there.** For the mechanism
  under test that is the same thing — the frame the removal reads is the master
  either way — but a beard that came with the original photograph is not what
  was measured.
- **Identity was not judged.** The horns court asked *"same person"* on every
  frame; this one asked only about the beard and the skin under it. The frames
  look like the same man to me, and that is an impression rather than a reading.
- **No per-side arm**, because a beard has no sides. The mirror hazard that
  costs pairs their court does not arise here.

## What this admits

```
facialHair may be REMOVED, by the vacancy road, on the repaint road
the phrase-to-kind move is proved by the first kind that needed it
marks and ink follow only after their own runs — no verdict spreading
```

The two kinds behind it are the same pattern and a different picture: `marks`
folds into the `skin` slot and would need its own phrase and its own reading;
`ink` has no slot at all until the tattoo studio gives it one (D-138). Neither
is promoted by this run, per fable-531 §4c.
