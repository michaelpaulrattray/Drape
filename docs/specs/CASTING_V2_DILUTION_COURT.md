## §0 The evidence, and the sentence this is judged against

**His demonstration** (2026-08-25). He took his own 553-character brief, sent it
straight to GPT Image 2 as a single prompt, and put the result beside our sheet:

> *"the issue we are having is our guys are all combing out looking like
> bodybuilders... they dont look as accurate as the gpt image 2 prompt delivered
> alone e.g they all have the same facial hair for some reason a similar body
> type, no body cybernetics like it just isnt making sense from a cast stand
> point."*

His raw prompt returned porcelain skin, gaunt severe bone structure, a large
mechanical jaw plate, visible scalp seams and ports, an amber-red eye, no beard,
not muscular — **his brief, painted with conviction.** Our five delivered frames
returned near-identical bearded muscular men with ordinary fair skin, dot-sized
implants, no visible jaw plate and no discernible amber eye.

**The governing sentence** (fable-1662, his words):

> *"obviously our compiler has a lot of important things in it but also it cant
> be as the cost of creativity this is especially true for initial casting
> sheets. people are wanting to look at a casting sheet and decide we cant call
> ourselves the midjourney o[f] casting if we can't be as creative as
> midjourney"*

**And its addendum** (fable-1663, which closes the reading 1662 leaves open):

> *"if someone does ask for extremely specifics obviously deliver it but it needs
> that creative flare which sells the vision or imagination they had"*

**Specificity does not downgrade a brief to transcription.** The contract on a
maximally-specific ask is BOTH halves: every stated fact delivered exactly, AND
painted with the conviction that sells the vision behind the words — his jaw
plate, not our dot implants. On a specific ask, creative flare lives in
EXECUTION (boldness, materiality, drama of the stated things); on an open ask it
also lives in INVENTION.

**And the third leg** (fable-1667, his words):

> *"it cant break us genuinely creating realistic humans though this is one thing
> the engine is already great at."*

**So the scaffolding is not presumed guilty.** Some of those 134 shared sentences
are WHY ordinary sheets come back reliably photoreal, identity-consistent and
refusal-safe. The ablation tells us what a sentence COSTS in conviction on a
fantastical brief; this constraint says the burden runs the other way on an
ordinary one. **A repair that buys the cyborg his jaw plate by costing an
ordinary model sheet its realism is refused whatever the strip looks like.** See
§3's control-arm requirement, and note where it binds: on the REPAIR, not on the
diagnosis.

**The court's success criterion, stated before it runs:** an ablation arm must
reach his-raw-prompt conviction at his eye while keeping the sheet's structure.
Anything less is not done.

### 1b — The facial hair. ⚠ THE AXIS VARIED AND THEN A LATER PASS COLLAPSED IT, AND THIS IS MEASURED RATHER THAN INFERRED

**The pass whose job is to break twins makes facial hair LESS varied.** Its
mechanism is in `realizedAxes.ts`: when a candidate's beard BUCKET (`bare` or
`bearded`) is already present among its neighbours, it re-picks from the OTHER
bucket's weighted pool — and for a man in his 40s the `bearded` pool is
dominated by `short beard` (17 of the bucket's ~36 weight, ~47%). **So every
forced flip toward `bearded` lands on `short beard` about half the time**, and
the sheet concentrates on the one value.

⚠ **AND IT FIRES ONLY ON A BRIEF THAT STATES THE HAIR — 0 of 16,000 values move
on an ordinary brief, against 22.8% on his.** That is by design and the design
says so: facial hair is the twin-breaker's FALLBACK axis, reached when the hair
rules stand down because the brief authored the hair. The file's own docblock
names the female version of this as a limit it cannot fix (*"a sheet of women
whose hair the brief genuinely stated cannot be separated at all"*). **The male
version is the mirror and nobody measured it: the fallback fires, and the
separation it buys costs more variety than it returns.**

**His brief says "Bald."** That one word is what routed his sheet into this
lane.

⚠ **His 5-of-8 is still at the tail of the repaired model (0.3%, about one sheet
in three hundred), so the pass explains the DIRECTION and does not fully explain
his draw.** Said rather than rounded away. What is not in doubt: the axis is
moved 22.8% of the time by a pass that reduces its spread.

## §4 The two repairs that do NOT need this court

Both are named here so they are not re-derived, and neither ships without its own
countersign — a prompt change courts before it ships, and *context is not
additive*.

### 4a — The facial-hair twin-breaker (free to fix, free to verify)

The rule flips a clashing beard bucket and re-picks from the other bucket's
WEIGHTED pool, which concentrates on that pool's heaviest member. Three shapes
are available and the measurement above can grade all of them offline, at zero
cost, before a single frame is bought:

1. **Re-pick UNIFORMLY within the target bucket** rather than by weight — the
   bucket choice is already the taste decision, so weighting inside it only
   re-imposes the population prior the flip was overriding.
2. **Exclude values already on the sheet** from the re-pick pool, which is what
   the STYLE rule does one block up and what this rule conspicuously does not.
3. **Cycle rather than sample**, the way `energy` does — eight candidates against
   the bucket's members, one of each, offset per roll.

⚠ **Whatever is chosen is graded by re-running the census, not by argument.** The
bar is the BEFORE column: the pass must not reduce `distinct values per sheet`
below the raw draw's 5.27, and must not raise any single value above its declared
weight. A rule that breaks twins by making everyone the same has not broken
twins.

### 4b — The PHYSIQUE block versus his own stated face

His *"gaunt cheeks, severe bone structure, hard jawline"* and our *"thicker neck,
defined trapezius, firmer jawline — must be visible in the frame"* are two
instructions about one body, and ours is the one written as a requirement.

The precedence rule the product already states is that **stated facts outrank the
resolver** — the shared prompt says so in as many words: *"Every fact stated
there — sex, age, heritage, build — outranks the DIRECTION block, the LOOK, the
expression whisper."* That sentence names `build` and does not name the PHYSIQUE
block, which is the resolver's own voice speaking in the absolute register.

**Two candidate repairs, and the choice is a design decision rather than a
patch**: soften the physique register when `characterNotes` describe the body
(his gaunt cheeks are body description), or drop the physique line entirely when
the brief describes bone structure. Both are prompt changes on every cast in the
product, so both wait for a countersign and both are testable inside arm C, which
already removes the block.

⚠ **`Do not default to a slim runway physique` is the sentence to be most careful
with.** It exists for a measured reason — a bare build adjective was being
ignored and a blacksmith came back with a runway body — so removing it is
reopening a closed defect, not tidying. Arm C removes it along with the rest of
the block and that is what makes C an ABLATION rather than a proposal: it says
what the scaffolding costs, never that the scaffolding should go.
