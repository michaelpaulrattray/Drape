# The refine engine court — how many edits a picture takes before it falls apart

**Card #801 · run 2026-10-15 · three casts, two roads, one engine, about 160 renders.**
**His eye closes it (law 9): nothing below is a decision, it is what was measured.**

---

## In plain English, first

You asked how many edits we can make to one picture before it turns to mush,
if each edit is made on the last picture instead of going back to the original
every time. We cast three characters — two people and a lizard man — gave each
of them six ordinary edits of the kind a customer types, and ran the whole
thing twice: once through the studio exactly as it works today, and once the
new way.

**Three things came out of it, and the first one is about today, not about the
new way.**

**1 · Today's studio keeps the person beautifully and loses the edits.** On the
fisherman we asked for a scar; it arrived, and it was gone again one edit
later. We asked for gold earrings; they arrived, and they were gone on the
very next edit. On the young woman the haircut flipped back to the original hairstyle on
*every other* edit. The studio is doing what it was built to do — going back to
the original picture each time so the face cannot drift — and the machinery
that is supposed to carry the earlier edits forward did not carry a single one
across three chains.

**2 · The new way keeps everything.** Every edit stayed, including on the lizard
man: deeper scales, then horns, then amber slit eyes, then a torn ear — and
when we said *take the horns off*, the horns went and everything else stayed.
Six edits deep the character is still clearly the same character.

**3 · Nothing turned to mush.** The picture does drift — it gets a little
warmer and a little crunchier with each edit — but slowly and steadily, about
the same amount every time, with no cliff anywhere in six steps. It is colour
and texture creeping, not a picture falling apart.

**And it is cheaper and faster**: about 51 seconds and 2–3¢ an edit the new
way, against about 97 seconds and 5–7¢ today.

**Two things worth knowing before anything is built on this.** The new way has
no interpreter in front of it, so it has no refusals — it paints whatever it is
asked. And of eighteen perfectly ordinary edits, today's studio refused two of
them for free: *a small sparrow tattoo on the side of the neck*, and *a notch
missing from the left ear*. Those are the studio being rigid in exactly the way
your N3 note is about.

**Your Sign question is answered too, and the answer is better than the note
assumed.** After a chain of edits, the signed views come out as the **edited**
character — all five of them, on the lizard man. They do, because a Sign
anchors on the version the customer is looking at, not on the original.

## §1 · What was on trial

The ENGINE was held still. Every picture below was painted by
`openai/gpt-image-2.5/sunburst/edit`, which is what the product's repaint road
already sends to and what he settled on 2026-09-26 (*"anywhere we currently use
gpt image 2.0 will be 2.5 sunburst by default"*) and again on 2026-10-15
(*"no need to test flare sunburst is the one"*).

What varied is the **ROAD** — where the edit is anchored, what rides with it,
and what the words say.

| cell | anchor | references | words |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A** control | the pristine master | the library's crops, as the product derives them | the whole instruction list replayed |
| **B** | the previous delivered frame | that frame only | the new ask alone |
| **C** | the previous delivered frame | that frame **and the master** | the new ask alone |
| **D** | the previous delivered frame | that frame and the master | the new ask **and a one-line state note** |
| **E** | the previous delivered frame | that frame and the master | **the whole instruction list replayed** |
| **F**, **G** | — | identical to C in every byte | — the floor |

- **B against C** answers his (b): does the master as a second reference help?
- **C / D / E** answers his (c): do the words have to carry the history?
- **A against the best of B–E** is the headline.
- **F and G are C run again**, so no cell is ranked against another until the
  gap clears the spread between three identical runs.

⚠ **C, D and E each send one sentence B does not** — *"The first image is the
picture to edit. The second image is the original photograph of the same
person, for likeness only"* — because a second picture with nothing said about
it is not an arm; the engine blends it. That is a confound and it is named here
rather than discovered later.

⚠ **Cell A is the product and B–G are not.** A goes through `refineCandidate`,
the real paid entrance, on the dev database: the interpreter, the walls, the
charge, the reference library, the repaint compositor, the verification reader.
B–G drive the same engine factory directly, because **the product has no
incremental road to drive** — that road is what the court exists to price.

## §2 · The fixtures, and why they are these

Three casts, open and varied, two people and one creature — his *"humans and
creatures"*. Each chain is six asks written the way a customer writes, with a
**removal of an earlier edit at step 5**, which is the case the incremental
road has no mechanism for: the thing to be taken away exists only in the
picture.

| fixture | brief | the six asks |
|---|---|---|
| **fisherman** (cand 728) | *A weathered fisherman, north Atlantic, sixty* | beard shorter and neater · a scar through the left eyebrow · the eyes grey-green · small gold hoop earrings · **take the scar off** · hair darker, more salt than pepper |
| **casting** (cand 744) | *A street casting, mid 20s* | cut the hair into a short crop · a small sparrow tattoo on the side of the neck · wire-frame glasses · freckles across the nose and cheeks · **take the glasses off** · the eyes pale grey |
| **lizard** (cand 736) | *A lizard man with emerald scales, a heavy brow and amber eyes, in their 40s* | scales a deeper emerald · a pair of short curved horns · amber eyes with slit pupils · a notch missing from the left ear · **take the horns off** · a heavier brow |

**The chains are WRITTEN, and that is a measurement rather than a preference.**
His reshape says *"use his real edit histories where they exist"*, so they were
read first, on production: **`casting_candidate_variants` holds TWO rows, all

his nose less human"*. **There is no real chain anywhere to copy.** The
question this card asks has never been exercised in production at all.

**Dev held no creature**, measured at the rows: of 86 dev rolls, three are
creature-ish and all three have zero frames. One was cast through the real
entrance for this court. **And dev's only other human fixture was a ghost** —
roll 132's eight candidates all carry one `imageKey` and that object is not in

## §3 · The instrument, and the controls it had to pass first

Four readings per frame pair, none of them chosen because it was easy:

| reading | what it is | why |
|---|---|---|
| `framing` | bbox and centroid of the subject silhouette, as a percentage of the frame | Sunburst's edit door re-renders the whole picture, so the first thing that moves on a chain is the camera. Seen on the very first probe, before any harness existed. |
| `dE` | CIEDE2000 **inside silhouette(a) ∩ silhouette(b)**, with the IoU printed beside it | a mean taken over two different extents is a number about the mask, not about the picture |
| `bgDE` | the same, **outside** both silhouettes | the part of the frame nobody asked about at all |
| `sharpPct` | variance of the Laplacian over one shared region, as a percentage of the master's | *turn to mush* is his word and this is the only reading that is about it directly |

**Nothing above was believed until the controls passed, and they are printed
with every run** (`scripts/_801-measure-disposable.mts --controls`):

- **CIEDE2000 against six published pairs** — 2.0425, 2.8615, 3.4412, 1.0000,
  1.2644, 4.3065, every one matched to under 0.001.
- **master against itself** — `dE 0`, `bgDE 0`, `IoU 1`, `sharp 100%`, framing
  all zero. Exactly zero, not nearly.
- **master against a σ=2 blur** — `sharp 0.9%`. It can see mush.
- **master against a known 16-pixel shift** — `framing.dx 1.56%` against the
  1.56% that was applied. It can see the camera move.

⚠ **The silhouette is cut from the backdrop, and both of its two bugs were
found by LOOKING at the mask rather than by reasoning about the number.** Seeded
from every border pixel, the fill started *inside* the sweater and ate the
clothing; seeded from the top and allowed to walk a local gradient, it walked
down the side of the casting fixture's arm and into a black vest, reading 28.7%
of the frame where the eye says 65%. It is local continuity **and** global
membership now, and the dumped masks are in `output/801/maskdump`.

## §4 · What happened — today's road

**16 of 18 open asks delivered; 2 answered for free without a render, nothing
charged.** Median wall time **97 s** (68–180 s). 500 ledger credits each, dev.

**It holds the person and the frame better than anything else in this court.**
On the fisherman, every delivered frame sat at `dE 2.4–3.0` against the master
with `IoU 0.990–0.996` and framing drift under a tenth of a percent — and it
stays there, because nothing accumulates: the tenth edit is as close to the
original as the first. That is the road working exactly as designed.

**And it loses the edits.** Read at the rows, not inferred:

- **The reference library minted ZERO crops across the fisherman's six-step
  chain**, and `carried: []` on every single render of all three chains. Two
  rows were minted in the whole court, both `hair` on the casting fixture, both
  recorded `disputed` — *"its crop is cut for a human, not for the library"*.
- So the replay carried everything by WORDS, and words lose the feature. Seen
  at the frames: the scar arrives at step 2 and is **gone at step 3**; the gold
  hoops arrive at step 4 and are **gone at step 5** (`fisherman-earring-lost.jpg`
  — native resolution, both ears); the casting fixture's haircut **flips between
  the bun and the crop on alternate steps**.

This is `build-is-lost-under-words` — a class this program has measured before
(words carry 0%, a padded crop 92–109%) — re-measured here on the shipped road,
on ordinary chains, with the carry that is supposed to prevent it firing not
once.

**One more thing the eye caught and the number agrees with.** On the fisherman's
step 5 — *take the scar off* — the whole **wardrobe changed**: the navy sweater
and yellow bib became a plaid shirt and grey overalls. `dE` jumped to **15.288**
against a chain that had been sitting at 2.4, then fell back to 3.014 at step 6
when the clothes came back. Today's road's instability is episodic rather than
cumulative, and it can rewrite anything it was not asked about.

**The two free refusals, both of them ordinary asks:**

- *"a small sparrow tattoo on the side of the neck"* → *a words-only tattoo ask
  with no measured place — answered before the claim, nothing spent.*
- *"a notch missing from the left ear"* → *That names one side of a pair, and
  pointing at it is how I can work on just that one — tap it on their picture
  and say it there.*

Both are deliberate doors with their own cards (#65, the side-phrasing road).
Both are named here because they are the rigidity his N3 principle is about,
and because **the incremental road painted both of them without hesitating.**

## §5 · What happened — the incremental road

**72 of 72 delivered** across cells B–E, and 12 of 12 on the floor cells.
Median wall time **51 s** (30–76). No interpreter, no walls, no credits.

**Every edit stayed.** The fisherman's scar survives to step 4 and goes only
when it is asked to go; the hoops arrive at step 4 and are still in both ears at
step 6 (`fisherman-incremental-faces.jpg`). On the lizard man
(`lizard-incremental-full.jpg`): deeper emerald → horns → amber slit eyes → a
torn ear, each one still there at the next step, and on *take the horns off* the
horns go and nothing else does.

**And it drifts, steadily.** Against the master, on the fisherman:

## §6 · His three questions, answered against the floor

**The floor first: three identical runs of cell C ended at 5.665, 5.768 and
5.970 — a spread of 0.305 ΔE at step 6.** No claim below is made on a gap
smaller than that.

**(a) Does Sunburst carry an edit forward in the picture itself?**
**Yes, and through all six steps.** Every edit of all three chains survived to
the end of its chain unless it was asked to leave. Nothing degraded to
unusable; the cost is a steady ≈0.6 ΔE per edit of colour and texture creep.
**Where it ends beyond six steps is unmeasured** — the curve is still linear at
six, so a longer chain is the obvious next reading and this court did not buy
it.

**(b) What to send as references.**
**The master as a second reference measurably helps.** B (previous frame only)
ends at **6.92**; the C-family (previous + master) ends at **5.80 ± 0.15**. The
gap is **1.12 ΔE against a floor of 0.31** — about 3.6× the noise. It costs one
extra reference, roughly **$0.008–0.012**, and about **16 seconds** (B median
40 s, C median 56 s). **Recommended: send both.**

**(c) What the words still need to do.**
**Nothing.** C (the new ask alone) **5.97**, D (plus a one-line state note)
**5.67**, E (the whole list replayed) **5.77** — all three sit INSIDE the
5.665–5.970 band that three identical runs of C produced. **Replaying the whole
instruction list helps nothing, costs prompt length, and is the half of today's
road that the picture makes unnecessary.** The state note is free and harmless;
on this evidence it is also unnecessary.

**(d) The Sign question.**
**The signed views come out as the EDITED cast.** Read at the code first —
`server/db/castingV2Sign.ts:321`, *"the variant wins where it exists"*: the
Sign's anchor is the selected variant's image, not the candidate's. Then seen
at the frames: an edited creature was signed and all five delivered views —
three-quarter, profile, close-up, full front, full back — carry the deep
emerald, the amber eyes and the heavier brow (`sign-views-of-an-edited-creature.jpg`).
**So the premise in the note — *"today they render from the master plus carried
feature words and ink crops"* — is half right: the words and crops ride, but
the ANCHOR is already the edited picture.**

**(e) And the image analyzer, which was his other sentence that day.**
The product's own face scan (`scanFace`, the same reader the panel uses) was
run on eight frames of these chains, and **its rows follow the edit**. Every
row below is in `output/801/reader/<fixture>.json`:

| frame | asked / found | the feature under test |
|---|---|---|
| fisherman master | 12 / 11 | **earring empty** — there is none |
| fisherman A-4 (today, right after the hoops) | 12 / 13 | **earring@left + earring@right boxed** |
| fisherman A-6 (today, two steps later) | 12 / 11 | **earring empty** — correct: the road had lost them |
| fisherman C-4 (incremental, after the hoops) | 12 / 12 | **earring@left + earring@right boxed** |
| fisherman C-6 (incremental, two steps later) | 12 / 11 | **still boxed, both ears** |
| lizard master | 12 / 4 | **horns empty** |
| lizard D-2 (after the horns) | 12 / 9 | **horns@left + horns@right boxed** |
| lizard D-6 (after *take the horns off*) | 12 / 4 | **horns empty** |

**An added feature appears, a removed one goes, and it does it on a creature as
well as on a person.** On the lizard it also, correctly, finds nothing for
`hair`, `facial hair` and `eyebrows` on every frame it managed to read.

⚠ **Its one real problem is transport, not reading: 4 of the 8 scans lost
questions to `fetch failed` — 12 questions in all, one scan losing six.** A
thinned panel is indistinguishable, on the panel, from a face that genuinely
has none of those features, and the lizard's master read 4 of 12 for that
reason alone. **That is a finding about the reader's reliability and it is F3
below, not a finding about this court.**

## §7 · The verdict, in the card's own vocabulary

The card pre-registered three verdict shapes. **This is (a), with the trade in
clause 3's direction rather than against it.**

> *(a) 2.5 holds the untouched region and identity across the chain → file the
> retirement of the replay/composite as its own measured rung, one workaround
> at a time, each behind its flag.*

**Cost and seconds, stated beside the quality finding, as clause 3 requires:**

| | today's road | the incremental road |
|---|---|---|
| wall time per edit | **97 s** median (68–180) | **51 s** median (30–76) |
| house cost per edit | **5–7¢** — one picture plus five Claude reads (#1689's reading) | **2–3¢** — one picture, two references, no text call |
| edits kept at step 6 | **the ones its words happen to hold** — 0 crops carried in this court | **all of them** |
| drift from the master at step 6 | **~3.0**, and it stays there forever | **~5.8**, growing ~0.6 per edit |
| ordinary open asks refused | **2 of 18**, free, nothing charged | **none — and that is not a win**: there is no interpreter in front of it to refuse with |

**It wins on quality-of-outcome, cost and speed at once**, which is rare enough
to be suspicious, so here is the honest statement of what it costs: **it trades
a guarantee for an accumulation.** Today's road can never drift, because it
never builds on itself. The incremental road never loses an edit, because it
always builds on itself.

⚠ **At ONE edit the two roads are the same picture** — step 1 reads 2.70 (A)
against 2.73 / 2.99 / 3.00 (C, F, G), which is inside the floor. The
difference is entirely a thing that appears with depth. **A customer who makes
one edit loses nothing today; a customer who makes six is not being served by
today's road at all.**

**What this court does NOT settle, and no card should be cut as though it did:**

1. **The walls.** The incremental road has no interpreter in front of it. Every
   refusal, every free answer, every bit of the money model lives on the road
   that A takes. Moving the renderer does not move those, and designing how
   they sit in front of an incremental render is a product decision.
2. **Beyond six edits.** The curve is still linear at the end of this court.
3. **The branch tree.** Today's variant tree lets a customer fork from any past
   edit. An incremental road re-renders from one frame, so forking means
   re-walking a branch; nothing here measured that.
4. **His eye.** Every number above is a pointer. The strips are the verdict.

## §8 · Findings that are not about the engine

Each of these is a product fact this court turned up and is **not** folded into
it — his 2026-09-24 ruling: a finding made while measuring something else is
its own card.

**F1 · The reference library carried nothing, on any chain.** 16 delivered
refines, 2 library rows, both `disputed`, `carried: []` every time. The
crop-carry is the mechanism that lets a master-anchored replay preserve earlier
edits, and in this court it never fired. This is #1577's territory (the refine
road's half of the ghost audit) and belongs on a card of its own.

**F2 · A `take the scar off` ask rewrote the wardrobe.** `dE 15.288` and a
different shirt, on the shipped road, on a frame a customer would be charged
for.

**F3 · The face reader's transport fails intermittently.** 4 of 8 scans lost
12 questions in all to `fetch failed`, one of them six at once — which turned
a creature's master into a 4-of-12 reading. A thinned panel reads, on screen,
exactly like a face that has none of those features.

**F4 · Two of eighteen ordinary open asks were refused** — both known doors,
both exactly the rigidity N3's design principle names.

## §10 · Instrument findings, including two of mine

**I1 · Two processes shared one manifest file and clobbered each other.** The
control run and the incremental run each wrote their whole in-memory copy on
every save, so the later writer erased the earlier one's rows. The FRAMES were
never at risk and both runs logged every step, so the record was rebuilt from
the logs (`scripts/_801-merge-disposable.mts`) — but the file that was supposed
to be the record was not one for about an hour.

**I2 · A killed background run replayed a chain onto itself, and that arm is
VOID.** The first creature control run hit its background time limit mid-chain;
the resumed process could not see the rows the other writer had clobbered and
re-ran step 1 **onto a live chain**, so the variant rows read *"deeper emerald …
horns … deeper emerald …"*. An arm whose road is not the road it claims is void
rather than failed, so the creature was cast again on a sibling candidate and
the whole first pass is excluded by name. Its frames are kept under
`output/801/frames/lizard-void-735` rather than deleted.

**I3 · The refine entrance returns a RECEIPT, not a frame, when
`CASTING_REFINE_DISPATCH_SCOPE` is on** — which it is on production. The first
control run read three of those receipts as delivered frames and wrote three
copies of the master to disk; the next step then met *"That edit is already
being made"*, and when the process exited the render was abandoned mid-flight.
The court runs with that flag off and reads the frame from the **variant row**,
which is the artifact either way. ⚠ **A Sign does the same thing one layer up**
— `schedulePackage ?? detach` — so the first Sign of this court charged 3,250
credits and left the cast at `provisioning` with no views ever rendered.
