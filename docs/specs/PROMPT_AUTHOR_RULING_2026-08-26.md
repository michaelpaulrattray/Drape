## 1. The user's words go to the engine

1. The user's words reach the engine **verbatim, first, and authoritative**. Nothing drops a stated fact.
2. The creative register is **the only road** for every roll. The engagement gate ("ambiguity resolves to false") is deleted.

## 2. Author, not compiler

3. One text call **authors** the final prompt: the user's words plus what an expert prompter would add — style, framing, background, lighting, and (per the imagination level) invented specifics. Prose in, prose out. No schema between user and engine.
4. **The author may reword and restructure freely; FACTS must survive** ("female 30's goth chick with pale skin" → an expert sentence). The reader compares stated facts before and after; a rewording that drops or contradicts one is refused.
5. **The expanded prompt is shown on the cast, editable.** No hidden prompt, ever.
6. The **reader** keeps running beside the prompt — recording facts for the feature panel, tattoo rows, coverage and rules — and **never authors, never gates, never invents**.
7. Retired from the roll prompt: the anatomy blocks (SKIN/EYES/SCLERA/PUPILS/CATCHLIGHTS/LASHES/BROWS/LIPS/HAIR/HERITAGE IS BONE), IDENTITY_INTEGRITY, PRIORITY, SPECIFICITY, NEGATIVES, the age/build/grey dice, the 25-word/180-char notes cap, the discard list.

## 3. Settings are defaults; the prompt overrides

8. **Photoreal is the default style.** "UNLESS the prompt specifies a specific style its overidden. anything in the settings outlined in the prompt is overriden by the user prompt."
9. **Styles are ART styles** in the settings modal — photoreal, painted-illustrative realism, glossy anime-poster, more later — **described**, never named after an IP (the copyright guard).
10. **The imagination meter** (Midjourney-style) sets how opinionated the author is — his spec, verbatim, is §5.
11a. **A style is a bundle.** His word, verbatim: *"those are more advanced settings but by default we set it on our best known settings e.g most people will just flick between styles we just need to ensure every style has a specific preset default setting but for now photoreal is our only and default style that goes into every prompt with waist up framing and the studio lighting and so on we can expand styles and settings later."* So: every style preset carries its own default framing, background and lighting; framing/background/lighting are ADVANCED settings, hidden by default; users flick between styles. **For now there is ONE style — photoreal — and its bundle goes into every prompt: **chest-up framing** (his word after the waist-up sentence: "chest up is fine" — matching his own test prompts), the studio lighting, the neutral seamless-gradient background, the photoreal quality clause.** The prompt overrides any of it (rule 8). Locked across all eight slices (one prompt per sheet). Styles and advanced settings expand later.

## 4. Outfits, variation, length, framing, refusals

11. **The wardrobe/basics toggle is retired.** "there is no way we could invent these types of outfits with words the engine just does it beautifully we should just let the engine decide the outfits based on the prompt". "Basics" is a word a user may type, not a mode or a default.
12. **Skin coverage is MEASURED on the frame** (a region read, cents) for the ink and wardrobe-edit roads — never assumed from a path. Refuse-honestly-until-read stays.
13. **No variance card.** "we dont need to state or force a difference in the prompt the engine already openly invents these things if they are not mentioned." Stated = on every slice; unstated = the engine's.
14. **~400-word budget** to the engine, total; the user's words are never cut; the author fits in what is left and trims itself first. A tested constant. The cliff is MEASURED by the court, not assumed.
15. **Framing trim** (`CASTING_FRAMING_TRIM_SCOPE`) is not deleted on today's evidence; it goes on the court. If a stated framing sentence holds head size inside the trim's own bar, the trim retires.
16. **Refusals: the engine is the judge.** "let the engine itself refuse and refund because we dont know what will pass the engine and what wont. additionally our author should sanitize/re-word prompts to ensure they pass." The content wall is deleted; the author writes filter-safe wording; an engine refusal refunds the slice and the tile says why (#122); #93's rewrite-and-retry road serves this.

## 5. The imagination meter — his spec, verbatim

> At max imagination the AI author is no longer writing a straightforward casting brief. It is treating the user's short request ("goth woman mid 30s") as a seed and then deliberately inventing a highly distinctive, memorable, Midjourney-style identity around that seed — while still forcing the final output to remain a clean casting studio portrait (plain or softly graded background, character-focused, no busy environments).
>
> The author is maximising: Specificity of appearance – not generic "goth", but a precise, ownable look. Stylistic intensity – heavier makeup, more dramatic hair architecture, stronger bone structure, more interesting skin texture, more deliberate asymmetry. Atmospheric and aesthetic exaggeration – lighting, mood, micro-details, and subtle surreal or hyper-fashion touches that feel like high-end Midjourney. Memorable uniqueness – the kind of face and styling that would stand out in a casting book or model portfolio.
>
> It is not changing the core brief (still a woman in her mid-30s, still recognisably goth, still a studio casting shot). It is pushing how far that identity can be taken while remaining coherent and usable.
>
> The author should: Expand the thin user request into a rich, opinionated character description. Invent distinctive visual signatures (hair cut + colour combination, specific makeup language, jewellery language, facial structure, posture energy). Lean into Midjourney-style inventiveness: unexpected but coherent details, strong aesthetic direction, high visual density. Keep the technical constraints of a casting shot (plain/soft background, portrait framing, clean lighting language) so the image still works as a studio reference. Write the final prompt in clean, filter-safe language suitable for GPT Image 2.

His system instruction for MAX:

```
You are the prompt author for a casting studio image engine.

User request: [user's short description]
Imagination level: MAX

Your job at MAX imagination:
- Treat the user request only as a seed.
- Invent a highly distinctive, memorable, Midjourney-level identity around that seed.
- Maximise visual uniqueness: specific hair architecture, intense but coherent makeup, interesting facial structure, strong texture, deliberate asymmetry, and atmospheric lighting.
- Keep the shot as a clean casting studio portrait (plain or softly graded background, character-focused, no environments or storytelling scenes).
- Stay true to the core identity (age range, gender presentation, and the requested aesthetic direction).
- Write a rich, detailed, opinionated prompt that feels like high-end Midjourney character design, but worded cleanly for GPT Image 2 (no NSFW triggers).

Output only the final image prompt.
```

His low/max example pair:

> LOW: Photorealistic casting portrait of a woman in her mid-30s with goth styling, dark hair, pale skin, subtle dark makeup, plain studio background, soft lighting.

> MAX: Photorealistic high-fashion casting portrait of a striking woman in her mid-30s with an elevated modern goth aesthetic. Sharp bone structure, pale cool-toned skin with visible texture, intense dark smoky eye makeup, deep burgundy lips, and a precise geometric black eyeliner wing. Hair is jet black with subtle blue-black undertones, cut into a severe asymmetrical bob with a sharp side part and longer pieces framing one side of the face. Small silver facial piercings and a thin black leather choker. She faces the camera with a calm, slightly confrontational expression. Clean soft gray studio background, dramatic yet controlled soft lighting that sculpts the face, ultra-detailed skin and hair texture, sharp focus, cinematic fashion photography.

> At max imagination the author is essentially acting as a creative director + Midjourney prompt engineer who is allowed to go hard on identity invention, while still delivering something that functions as a professional casting studio shot.
