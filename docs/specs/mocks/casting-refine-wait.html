The refining wait

Twenty-five credits, forty to ninety seconds, and a face you have already decided you like. The wait's job is to feel supervised — and the material for that is sitting right there: the picture being edited is on the screen, and it is literally what the edit is anchored to.

02Why the picture, and why blur

A refinement is edit(the original, your instructions) — every version is made from the face you are looking at, never from the last version. So the picture is not decoration during the wait. It is the input, and softening it says the only true thing there is to say: this is the material, it is being worked on, it is not finished.

The dots are the aliveness, and they are deliberately dumb. A grid of them lies over the whole frame and a slow wave passes through, on the same 4px grid everything else sits on. Full-frame and uniform on purpose: a highlight travelling over a picture captioned "the hair" would claim the model is working that region, which is not how any of this works. A field of dots claims only that something is laid over the picture until it is ready.

Nothing here ever speeds up. Between the moment the job is sent and the moment it lands we receive nothing at all — so motion that appeared to react would be inventing a signal. It moves the same way whether the answer is four seconds or four minutes away.

Colour drains while it waits and returns when it lands. That is the whole arrival moment, and it costs nothing to be honest about: an unfinished thing is grey, a finished one is not.

03"Honest progress", and what honest leaves us

D-169 asks for honest progress. Here is everything the pipeline actually knows, end to end:

There is no percentage available, and there never will be. Not withheld — absent. The provider tells us nothing between sending and landing, so a bar tracking "how much of the picture is done" would be a lie told slowly, and the one thing worse than a wait is a wait that turns out to have been lying.

But there is a real number, and it is a good one. The last 64 refines on production:

That is tight enough to say something true and useful. What it is not tight enough for is a bar. I built one — filling toward 31 seconds, holding near the end, never completing — and cut it, for a reason that survives its own cleverness: the endpoint was measured but the shape of the fill was invented, and a bar sitting inside that pill is read as a measurement of this job when it is a statistic about other people's. A sentence can carry "usually" honestly. A bar cannot carry it at all.

So the A / B switch beside the state list is about words:

  • A — say nothing. The dots and the two state words carry the whole wait. Nothing on screen can be mistaken for a measurement, because nothing is measuring.
  • B — say "usually about half a minute." One quiet line under the state. It is true, it is what you actually want to know at second five, and it is the same register as every other honest line in this product — including the two-minute one, which is this sentence's other half.

My recommendation is B. A wait with no shape at all is the thing you called flat, and "about half a minute" gives it a shape without pretending to watch it. If it turns out to over-promise on a slow day, the two-minute sentence is already there to take it back, and it says the credits come home.

Elapsed time stays off screen either way: a counter climbing past a minute makes an ordinary wait feel like a fault. Time earns a place at the two-minute mark, as the sentence in state 03.

04One voice, three jobs

A question, a refusal and an outcome are the same surface saying different things — so they are the same shape, the same size, the same distance from the box. Nothing here is a modal, an alert, or a red box: the product is not scolding anybody, it is talking.

Outcome

Refusal
Refining changes the person, not the shoot — a silk slip dress is a garment, a prop or the set, which comes after Sign. Jewellery, glasses and piercings do work here. Nothing was charged.

The chips retire a sentence. Both questions currently end by naming their own answers — "Say the hair, the eyes, makeup" — which was the stopgap for having no chips. With chips it is repetition, so it goes. Typing the answer keeps working exactly as it does now; the box being live is what says so, not a clause advertising it.

05Two at once

More than one running Legal today — nothing stops a second refine while the first is out.

The picture can only be one thing at a time, so it does not try: it softens once and means this face has work out, not this particular edit is happening. The pills stack, newest on the left, one per job. Each one is a separate 25 credits and a separate picture, and two pills is the cheapest way to say that before both land at once.

06What this costs to build

The treatment itself is client-only. Two small server additions carry the rest, and both are facts the row already holds:

  • The stage. status is already queued then dispatched on the variant row; the pending projection just doesn't return it. One field.
  • The drawer. The instruction is read before the charge, so the claimed row holds where it filed. filedSubjectsOf already exists and is already returned for finished versions — the same call, one line up. It is what would let the pill read "pinker — the hair · hair colour · being drawn", and it is the one piece I would not ship without your eye on it: it is the system explaining itself, and D-162 already moved that kind of talk out of the way once.

Nothing here needs a new table, a new poll, or a provider we do not have.

07What I want you to decide

Is the blur enough, or does the wait want one more thing?

The whole design rests on a picture going soft and a two-word status. It is restrained on purpose — but "restrained" and "still lame" are one bad judgement apart, and you are the one who called the current one flat.

My recommendation: ship it as it stands here. The thing that makes Grok's wait feel alive is that it is about your request, and this is too — your words, your face, going soft and coming back in colour. If it needs more after you have watched a real ninety-second one, the next honest increment is the drawer line, not motion.
