The brief echo

What the sheet says back to you after a brief compiles. It replaces the row of pills. One sentence, in the same type as everything else, with the facts the system pinned underlined — click one to change it or let it vary.

01What the sentence is made of

Your own words are not repeated back. They are still in the brief box above, and a machine paraphrase of your sentence is the thing that makes software feel like it is talking about you rather than working for you. The echo covers only what the system did: what it pinned across all eight, what it left free, and how the eight differ.

Two clauses carry the whole law. Held means everyone on the sheet has it. Left to the roll means it varies. The compiler checks every pinned fact against every candidate it composes, and refuses the roll if one drifts — so the sentence is a true account of what was sent to be photographed. It is not a claim about what came back. Judging the pictures is still your job.

Details in your own words — a shaved head, soot in the creases, freckles she never covered — are not restated. They reach every candidate as you wrote them, and the echo covers only the facts the system pinned from its own vocabulary. Saying your words back is padding; paraphrasing them is worse.

02Four sheets, four shapes of sentence

The point of composing rather than templating: a fuller brief and an emptier one do not produce the same sentence with different words in the gaps — they produce differently shaped sentences. Click any underlined fact.

03Against what is there now

Now
runway model female 20s slim dry East Asian
Proposed

Everyone on this sheet is a slim woman in her early 20s, of East Asian heritage, reading dry. The eight differ by look.

The same six facts either way, so this is not about density. The row makes you read them as data and assemble the person yourself, and it cannot say the one thing that matters most: that build was pinned to slim while look was deliberately left to vary. To a pill those are the same kind of thing. The sentence has somewhere to put the difference.

04The rules underneath

RuleWhy
Sex and age fuse "a woman in her early 20s" — never two facts in a row. With no sex pinned the subject becomes "the eight".
Build folds in An adjective on the noun, not an item: "a slim woman", not "a woman, slim".
Name open axes up to three "heritage, build and presence were left to the roll" is a sentence. Six of them is the pill row wearing a coat — past three it collapses to "nothing pinned".
Your own details stay yours A shaved head or soot in the creases is not restated. It reaches every candidate as written; the echo covers only what the system pinned from its own vocabulary.
Never name the same thing twice If presence is varying and the eight differ by disposition, that is one fact. It is named once, in the closing clause.
Everyday words, not field names energy reads as presence, variationAxis as how the eight differ. The vocabulary the system stores is not the vocabulary a person casts in.
The sentence never says "I" Verbs describe the sheet, never the model reading your brief.
Adjusting never changes this sheet Rolls are immutable. Every change says "applies to your next roll", exactly as unpinning does today.
Only closed vocabularies The popover offers the values the compiler can actually honour. Free text belongs in the brief box, where it gets interpreted.

05One thing to decide

The casting tab has no pill row to replace.

The pills only exist on the sheet, because interpretation happens when a roll is dispatched — the tab has nothing compiled to echo yet. Putting a sentence there means interpreting your brief as you type, which costs a call per pause, adds a wait before the button you actually came to press, and shows a paraphrase rewriting itself under your cursor.

Recommended: the echo lives on the sheet only, as a record of what the roll did. On the sheet's next-roll box it becomes a short forward line computed with no extra calls — "Next roll: presence unpinned, age set to 40s. Your words can still add pins." If you do want the tab to tell you what it heard before you spend credits, that is a different feature — interpret, then confirm, then roll — and worth deciding on its own rather than as a side effect of this one.

06What building it costs, and what it leaves out

Choosing a value needs a change the server does not have yet. A roll today takes your brief and a list of things to unpin — there is no way to say "make it 40s". So "Let age vary" would work on day one and every other option in these popovers would be dead. I am building the missing half rather than shipping a control that only half works, and it brings one real decision with it: an adjustment you made by hand has to outrank the interpreter's reading of your words, or your next roll quietly undoes it.

  • Editing a heritage blend's percentages. The popover replaces a heritage or lets it vary; a two-component 60/40 composer is the heaviest control here and earns nothing yet.
  • Pinning a fact the brief never mentioned and the system never derived. The brief box is where facts come from; the popover adjusts what is already on the sheet.
  • Anything on the casting tab, pending your answer above.
