# The whole-skin carrier — ship requirements, gathered before the decision

> **FOUNDER RULING, 2026-08-15 (fable-562): skin stays WORDS-ONLY until the
> tattoo studio launches.** *"Until the tattoo studio is launched we carry skin
> as words only otherwise it starts to become extremely complicated."*
>
> S does not ship. It is banked, not abandoned: the court, both laws it
> produced, and every clause below stand in the record, and the carrier is
> re-decided **on top of** the tattoo studio's pixel-authority rule (§7) rather
> than before it. His reasoning is sequence over complication, and shipping the
> cutout first would have baked an ambiguity the studio would then inherit.
>
> What it costs meanwhile is on the record and in the reliability watch: a paid
> tan drifts ~ΔE 5 per subsequent edit under words. Honest state, not a
> regression.

*Not a design and not a commitment. The arm (S) is measured
(`V4_TONE_CARRIER_COURT.md`), and this file collects what the design MUST answer
whenever it is revisited — written while the reasons are fresh rather than
reconstructed afterwards. Sources are named on every clause.*

---

## 1. The reference is re-cut from the newest delivered frame, never carried stale

*(fable-559, from the founder's own question about tattoos.)*

The front's law is that **a crop holds the region it depicts**. A whole-skin
cutout therefore pins whatever is ON that skin — including ink, which is a
benefit, since a tattoo drifts under words exactly as a tan does.

Both edge cases are staleness, and both are already-known diseases wearing a new
coat:

```
ink ADDED after the mint      the stale cutout shows bare skin and FIGHTS the
                              new tattoo — the carrier arguing with the ask
ink REMOVED after the mint    the stale cutout PAINTS IT BACK — the one-frame
                              removal, through a door nobody had opened
```

So the mint policy is the build carrier's own (`remint: "everyRender"`, ruled in
fable-424 §4): re-cut from the frame in hand rather than enumerating which edits
invalidate a skin crop. **And a removal render becomes the new source in the same
pass** — survival-of-the-absence applies to the reference, not only to the
recipe.

## 2. The label is a property sample, never an extent claim

*(fable-409 §1, and the catalogue's own `skin` note.)*

A face cut filed as "her skin" is a partial wearing the name of the whole. Arm S
is not a partial — the extent claim is TRUE of a whole-skin cutout — but the
label still says what it is, because the next cut somebody writes may be a
partial again and the wording is what stops it drifting.

## 3. The measurement that fires the mint reads the same pixels

*(fable-556 §4, from the mask-stability finding.)*

Any "did the tone actually change" test that gates a mint takes both means
inside `mask(v1) ∩ mask(v2)` and quotes the IoU. A mint that fires on a measured
delivery must not be measuring where a mask decided her neck begins — one face
in three moved up to 3.0 ΔE for that reason alone.

## 4. The scope gate, and what it costs on a face-dominant frame

*(fable-552 §3b.)*

- The carrier lands dark behind its own flag, additive, with no row written and
  no recipe reading one while it is off.
- **The open question the design must price**: a whole-skin cutout on a frame
  where skin is most of the picture is close to a second portrait of her. The
  identity column measured E inside the bar on 2 of 3 faces — the third,
  fair-short, at 1.92 against a bar of 1.755 — so the cost is small but not
  zero, and a tighter framing than this bench's may change it.

## 5. What it does NOT cover, stated so nobody assumes it

- **Freckles and placed marks.** They are facial and placed; a below-chin or
  whole-skin sample carries the tone, not the constellation. The freckle court
  stands short and waits on the marks machinery (D-138), not on this.
- **The tattoo studio handoff.** When ink gets first-class slots, the skin
  cutout and the ink references will cover the same pixels. Who owns what the
  cutout shows is a design section the studio work inherits — named here
  (fable-559 §4) so it is not discovered then.

## 7. The collision the studio must resolve first — and it is now the gate

*(fable-561, from the founder's own follow-up; and fable-562, which makes it the
condition of any revisit.)*

When ink arrives as flash-sheet references, **two references claim the same
patch of skin**: the flash sheet's crisp canonical design, and the cutout's
delivered pixels. Two candidate shapes, both to be COURTED in the studio's
design rather than argued:

```
ONE AUTHORITY PER PIXEL   an ink site with its own reference is CUT OUT of the
   (the early lean)       skin cutout — tone owns everything except ink sites,
                          the flash sheet owns the ink. `slotTwiceReferenced`'s
                          spirit widened from one reference per SLOT to one
                          reference per PATCH OF SKIN.
BIRTH VS CARRY            the flash sheet is authority at creation and edit
                          (born crisp); the cutout carries it between edits,
                          with photocopy-softening as the named risk and the
                          flash sheet as the periodic re-anchor.
```

The collision is measurable — ink crispness and tone hold, on the same frames,
under both shapes — so it is a court, not a debate. **No ink references exist
today, so the cutout currently collides with nothing**; the rule must exist
before the STUDIO ships, and now also before the carrier is re-decided.

## 6. The verdict this is contingent on

Arm S did **not** clear its pre-registered bar (median R 1.95 against 1.66; on
the same-pixel reading 1.72 against 1.66, a 0.06 miss inside the instrument's own
±0.07 agreement band). The decision in front of the founder is whether halving
the drift — ΔE ~5 under words to ΔE ~2 — is worth a crop per render. **If he says
no, this file is the record of what was ready rather than a plan that was
abandoned.**
