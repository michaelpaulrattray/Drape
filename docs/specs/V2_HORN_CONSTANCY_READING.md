# Are they the same horns? — the axis the survival court never judged

*Read 2026-08-15 on frames already rendered and paid for, ordered by fable-566
§1. **$0.09 of house money, 18 segmenter reads, no generations.** Artifacts:
`output/horn-constancy/`.*

---

## The founder's ruling and what it exposed

> *"Horns should be carried by reference as well — it's a feature, otherwise
> they would change on every refinement."*

The survival court measured **presence** (horns still there: 3/3 both arms) and
**identity** (still the same woman: 3/3 both arms), then broke the tie toward
words because words is cheaper. It never asked whether they are the same
**horns** — curve, extent, placement. That is the unowned-axis-collapse class:
an axis nobody pins re-rolls every render, and a court that cannot see it crowns
the cheaper arm.

## What the numbers say — and they do not separate the arms

Every reading is normalised against her own face on the same frame, because her
head does not sit in the same pixels between renders.

```
specimen     arm     extent drift   aspect drift
words-2      words        2%             1%
words-2      crop         2%             0%
words-3      words        8%             3%
words-3      crop        10%             3%
words-4      words        2%             2%
words-4      crop         1%             1%
```

**The crop arm holds extent and aspect nearer on 1 of 3** — the bar was 2 of 3.
On this evidence the two arms are indistinguishable.

## And the instrument cannot see laterality, which is stated rather than hidden

The third axis — where the horns sit, in face-widths — swung by **0.81** on two
specimens, which is a horn crossing to the other side of her head. It did not:
asked *"horns"*, the segmenter answers with **one horn**, and not always the same
one. That is the answer-a-class-with-an-instance behaviour this program has met
before, and it makes the placement axis unusable here.

The extent and aspect numbers survive it (a single horn of similar size reads
similarly), and the eye settles the rest.

## What the eye says — `output/horn-constancy/horn-sheet.png`

Parent beside child, three specimens, both arms:

- **words-3 and words-4: the same horns**, to a careful look — same length, same
  curve, same taper.
- **words-2: they re-rolled.** The parent's horns are shorter and thicker with
  the tips curling inward; both children carry longer, more slender horns with
  the tips turned out. Same *kind* of horn, not the same horns — and **the crop
  arm re-rolled with it**, which is the finding that matters for the arm
  question.

## The verdict this reading supports

**The founder's ruling stands on construction, not on this measurement** — which
is exactly what fable-566 §1 anticipated. A feature must be pinned by
construction rather than by luck, and three specimens where one visibly re-rolled
under BOTH arms is not evidence that words are safe; it is evidence that this
court cannot tell.

Two things follow, and they are the ordered work rather than this note's opinion:

1. **The survival battery gains a standing INSTANCE-CONSTANCY arm** — asked of
   every future kind, because a court that cannot see re-rolling will crown
   words again.
2. **That arm needs a better instrument than this one.** A paired feature is
   read per side (`regionSides`) or compared crop-to-crop; a class word returns
   one instance and hides the pair.

## The limits

- **Three specimens, one face, one chained edit each** — the survival court's
  own material, re-read rather than re-bought.
- **The crop arm's carrier was a horn crop of 93×235 to 121×259 px.** Whether a
  larger or differently-cut crop pins the instance is not tested here.
- **No judgement of which horns are better** — only whether they are the same
  ones.
