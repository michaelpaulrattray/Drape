# N3 — the refine era completes: the plan, in order (2026-10-15)

**Card: #2535.** Written by a relay agent on the founder's word (chat, 2026-10-15, verbatim and
entire): *"yes write the n3 plan"*. N3 opened the same day on *"start on n3"* (recorded on

## 1 · What the customer gets, in their terms

- **Looking at a cast is just the picture.** Nothing loads, nothing looks stuck.
- **The moment they mean to edit, a drawer slides out and fills feature by feature**, each one
  lighting up on the face as it arrives. Typing never waits for it.
- **The drawer lists what this cast actually has** — a third eye, jaw hardware, a mole, a collar —
  not a fixed list of thirteen human features. If something is not there, the drawer does not
  pretend it is.
- **The edit box is a real field the width of the picture**, with the price on its button, Undo
  only after an edit, and nothing to learn.
- **An edit holds the picture together** across many changes, on the best engine we measured.
- **A picture can be a reference by tapping the thing in it** (the Pinterest-style selector), and
  outfit cards show the piece, not just its name.
- **Refine stays cheap to run** — today it is 100 credits, and the face read under it gets cheaper
  still.

## 2 · The disappearing-technology gate (law clause 7), for the rung as a whole

1. **What must they learn?** Nothing. The drawer opens on the intent they already show (clicking
   the box, the face, or Refine). There is no scan button and no settings to read.
2. **What decision is put in front of them?** None new. The default engine for every job is ours
   to choose by measurement; no picker is added in N3.
3. **Where does the technology show?** It must not: no "scanning", no spinner text, no engine
   name, no confidence number. The arrival of features *is* the loading state (#2533).

### Phase B — How a face is read (#2532 → #30). The rung's detector.

What is measured (house money, reads only, $0.795 so far on #2532):

- **One discovery read names everything** — every feature on three of his casts, including a
  third eye, pointed ears and jaw hardware, and invented nothing on a plain cast. **$0.00909 a
  read, 7.5–11.8 s**, against today's **19–27 segmenter reads (median 22, ~11¢ at $0.005)**.
- **Its rough box is not usable** (1 of 12 boxes even contains the feature's centre). The names
  are the product; the outline must come from a segmentation read on demand (~½¢, mean 12.6 s).
- ⚠ **The hinge is unsolved:** the segmenter (SAM 3) returns **nothing** for the third eye and the
  jaw hardware under every wording tried — the two off-list features the rung exists for. A row
  the customer can read and not touch is not a feature.
- A pair (ears, earrings) can be answered in one read instead of two; the setting must be
  per-question (the shared `face` read breaks under it). Saves ~2.5¢ a scan; latency roughly
  neutral (reasoning, not measured).
- The second look at an empty read: **140 bought, 2 found something (1.4%), ~0.7¢ a scan.**

Steps:

1. **B1** find an engine that cuts what the discovery named (the card's candidate list: Grounding
   DINO, Florence-2, Moondream, SAM 2 automatic masks, OpenRouter vision models). Fixtures and
   ground truth are already on disk. **This decides whether the design works.**
2. **B2** carry a scan across versions (#2532 question 3). Needs fixtures made — production holds
   two candidate variant rows.
3. **B3** one recommended design on #2532, with frames, **for his approval before any build**.
4. **B4** build #30, the discovery detector, behind its own flag, dark on his account. The absent
   case is a first-class state of the panel (the #246 amendment). #1125 closes into it.

**Cost / latency:** projected panel open ~1¢ plus ~½¢ per feature touched, versus ~11¢ today —
**a projection from arms 2–3, not a measured scan.** First feature ~8–12 s; each cut ~12 s
(measured per read; whole-panel timing unmeasured). **Needs him:** the design in B3; the
second-look call (keep 0.7¢ a scan for a 1-in-70 rescue?); whether rows open as plain names and
fill in their cutout pictures as they are touched (arm 3's question 5 — a look he chose, so his).
