## 1 · What the customer gets, in their terms

- **Looking at a cast is just the picture.** Nothing loads, nothing looks stuck.
- **The moment they mean to edit, a drawer slides out and fills feature by feature**, each one
  lighting up on the face as it arrives. Typing never waits for it.
- **The drawer lists what this cast actually has** — a third eye, jaw hardware, a mole, a collar —
  not a fixed list of thirteen human features. If something is not there, the drawer does not
  pretend it is.
- **The edit box is a real field the width of the picture**, with the price on its button, Undo
  only after an edit, and nothing to learn.
- **An edit holds the picture together** across many changes, on the best engine we measured.
- **A picture can be a reference by tapping the thing in it** (the Pinterest-style selector), and
  outfit cards show the piece, not just its name.
- **Refine stays cheap to run** — today it is 100 credits, and the face read under it gets cheaper
  still.

## 2 · The disappearing-technology gate (law clause 7), for the rung as a whole

1. **What must they learn?** Nothing. The drawer opens on the intent they already show (clicking
   the box, the face, or Refine). There is no scan button and no settings to read.
2. **What decision is put in front of them?** None new. The default engine for every job is ours
   to choose by measurement; no picker is added in N3.
3. **Where does the technology show?** It must not: no "scanning", no spinner text, no engine
   name, no confidence number. The arrival of features *is* the loading state (#2533).

## 4 · The phases, in order

Each phase names what it depends on. **Step 0 goes first**, because its verdict decides which
parts of today's refine road survive. Alongside it, the retirements that are dead whatever the
court finds (A4 and A5) may proceed, and so may Phase C and E1, which are already approved and
being built. Everything else runs in the order shown.

### Step 0 — The engine court (#801) and the image analyzer (#2532), judged together. FIRST.

His words, 2026-10-15, chat: *"no need to test flare sunburst is the one"*; *"i dont think a
table of edits work thats was the old lane we now have a fully open casting studio so people can
cast creatures and all sorts of things right? like we cant be rigid"*; and *"obviously
everything has to work with the image analyzer which finds and boxs features right."*

- **Two arms:** today's refine road (the control) against **GPT Image 2.5 Sunburst `/edit`
  only**. No Flare arm.
- **Open, real edit chains, not an edit-kind table.** Asks written the way a customer writes
  them, as varied as the studio allows: humans and creatures, colour, shape, additions,
  removals, marks, hair and accessories. Use his real edit histories where they exist. The
  verdict is a general rule about the engine, not a lookup table.
- **Three questions, each measured on those chains:**
  - **(a) Carry in the picture.** With each edit applied to the previous delivered frame and the
    master as a reference, does Sunburst carry the earlier edits forward? How long does the
    untouched region and the identity hold across 5–6 edits, and when does it degrade?
  - **(b) Which references.** Master only; master plus the previous version; master plus the
    previous version plus a crop of the touched region. Cost (~$0.008–0.012 per reference) and
    latency beside each.
  - **(c) What the words still need to do.** If (a) holds, the instruction can be the new edit
    plus a short note on the current state. Measure whether replaying every earlier edit helps,
    does nothing, or hurts.
- **The signed-views question:** after a chain, do the **signed views** come out as the edited
  cast? Today they render from the master plus carried feature words and ink crops. Measure
  whether an edit that lives only in the picture reaches every view, a creature especially.
- **The image analyzer is judged on the same chains.** After every edit the reader runs on the
  new version; its rows and boxes must follow the edit (an added feature appears, a removed one
  goes, a changed one keeps its box); a masked edit uses the reader's own box for that feature;
  anything it misses or invents on an edited frame is reported beside the engine's frames.
- **Report:** cost and seconds per edit beside every frame. Runs without asking about spend
  (his standing rule); **his eye closes it** (law 9).

**What its verdict decides:** which parts of the reference-supplied refine road survive, how the
instruction is written (Phase D), and whether the signed views need the brief at all. **What it
does not gate:** A4 and A5 retire arms no account reaches, so they are dead whatever the court
finds, and they proceed in parallel. A1 and A2 also touch the refine road's machinery, so they
wait for the verdict rather than tidying code the verdict may rewrite (a recommendation of this
amendment, not his word).

**Cost / latency:** a Sunburst edit at `high` was **$0.0527 at 1024²** on fal (read 2026-09-24),
plus the references; the court's total and per-edit latency are **unverified until it runs**.

### Phase B — How a face is read (#2532 → #30). The rung's detector.

What is measured (house money, reads only, $0.795 so far on #2532):

- **One discovery read names everything** — every feature on three of his casts, including a
  third eye, pointed ears and jaw hardware, and invented nothing on a plain cast. **$0.00909 a
  read, 7.5–11.8 s**, against today's **19–27 segmenter reads (median 22, ~11¢ at $0.005)**.
- **Its rough box is not usable** (1 of 12 boxes even contains the feature's centre). The names
  are the product; the outline must come from a segmentation read on demand (~½¢, mean 12.6 s).
- ⚠ **The hinge is unsolved:** the segmenter (SAM 3) returns **nothing** for the third eye and the
  jaw hardware under every wording tried — the two off-list features the rung exists for. A row
  the customer can read and not touch is not a feature.
- A pair (ears, earrings) can be answered in one read instead of two; the setting must be
  per-question (the shared `face` read breaks under it). Saves ~2.5¢ a scan; latency roughly
  neutral (reasoning, not measured).
- The second look at an empty read: **140 bought, 2 found something (1.4%), ~0.7¢ a scan.**

Steps (the reader is also run on Step 0's edited frames, so every candidate is judged on edited
casts, creatures included, as well as on fresh ones):

1. **B1** find an engine that cuts what the discovery named (the card's candidate list: Grounding
   DINO, Florence-2, Moondream, SAM 2 automatic masks, OpenRouter vision models). Fixtures and
   ground truth are already on disk. **This decides whether the design works.**
2. **B2** carry a scan across versions (#2532 question 3). Needs fixtures made — production holds
   two candidate variant rows.
3. **B3** one recommended design on #2532, with frames, **for his approval before any build**.
4. **B4** build #30, the discovery detector, behind its own flag, dark on his account. The absent
   case is a first-class state of the panel (the #246 amendment). #1125 closes into it.

**Cost / latency:** projected panel open ~1¢ plus ~½¢ per feature touched, versus ~11¢ today —
**a projection from arms 2–3, not a measured scan.** First feature ~8–12 s; each cut ~12 s
